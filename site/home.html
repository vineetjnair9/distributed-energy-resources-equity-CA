Project Introduction

Distributed energy resource equity across California ZIP/ZCTA areas

This project builds a California ZIP/ZCTA-level dataset for studying how distributed energy resources are distributed across communities with different demographic, socioeconomic, climate, utility, and electricity-demand characteristics.

The site presents the data, paper, and exploratory tools behind a multi-DER analysis of rooftop solar PV, battery storage, EV charging, wind, and related controls. The goal is to make it easier to see where clean energy infrastructure is concentrated, where access appears limited, and how those patterns compare across regions.

What The Project Provides

A reusable data foundation for DER equity analysis

DER outcomes

The dataset cleans and aggregates source files for solar PV, EV chargers, EV stock, battery storage, wind, and small power plant records at the ZIP/ZCTA level.

Contextual controls

Each region is matched with ACS demographic and socioeconomic variables, NASA POWER climate and resource measures, utility-territory information, and electricity-demand controls.

Analysis-ready file

The main output is a combined DER dataset with predictors which is used for exploratory plots, regression models, robustness checks, and paper figures.

Paper

The Unequal Energy Transition: Racial and Socioeconomic Disparities in Distributed Energy Resource Adoption Across California

The accompanying paper asks whether race, income, poverty, education, climate, utility territory, and infrastructure context are associated with different levels of DER adoption across California communities.

Instead of treating each technology separately, the paper applies a consistent model ladder across solar PV, storage, EV charging, and supplementary wind outcomes. That structure makes it possible to compare whether inequity appears similarly across technologies or changes depending on the resource being studied.

The central contribution is both empirical and practical: the paper uses a shared dataset to test race-aware DER equity questions, while the repository preserves the processing and figure-generation workflow for future review or extension.

Exploring The Data

Ways to look at communities and compare regions

1

Inspect one region

Start with a single ZIP/ZCTA to view local DER outcomes alongside population, race and ethnicity shares, income, poverty, education, utility territory, demand, and climate variables.

2

Compare multiple regions

Put ZIP/ZCTA areas side by side to see whether differences in solar, storage, or charging align with demographic, socioeconomic, or infrastructure conditions.

3

Move from maps to models

Use choropleths and LOWESS plots for descriptive patterns, then compare those patterns with regression outputs and robustness figures from the paper.

Repository Guide

How the files fit together

The processing notebooks build the ZIP/ZCTA-level DER panel, add predictor data, and produce exploratory plots. The regression notebook runs the main model specifications, robustness checks, LOWESS plots, and model-comparison figures.

For a clean rebuild, scripts/rebuild_processed_data.py recreates the processed files from raw inputs. For refreshed paper outputs, scripts/run_all.py --only models figures executes the regression notebook and syncs figures into the site asset folder.
