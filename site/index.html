DER Adoption Disparities in California ZIP Codes

Author: Danielle Knutson Affiliation: MIT Last updated: (auto)

Abstract. This project studies how race/ethnicity and socioeconomic factors relate to distributed energy resource (DER) adoption across California ZIP codes. The primary outcome is installed DER capacity per capita, modeled as a function of demographic and socioeconomic predictors while controlling for contextual factors such as urbanization, renewable resource potential, electricity rates, and utility territory.

1 Introduction

This document outlines an equity-focused analysis of DER adoption across California ZIP codes. The goal is to quantify whether disparities in DER deployment persist across racial/ethnic and socioeconomic groups after accounting for income, education, homeownership, and other contextual factors that could confound adoption.

1.1 Motivation and Problem

DERs (e.g., rooftop/community solar, batteries, EVs, flexible loads) are growing quickly, but the data needed to study their deployment, equity impacts, and grid interactions is scattered across agencies, formats, and geographies. A core motivation of this work is to build a consistent, analysis-ready dataset that can support DER-rich grid studies and adoption modeling.

3.5b Regression Model Series

The core approach is interpretable regression. Models will be built incrementally: start with bivariate/baseline effects, then add controls, then fixed effects and interactions.

  • Baseline: $Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + \epsilon$ (one predictor at a time).
  • Full model (Income + Race + Controls): $Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1\text{Income} + \beta_2\%\text{Black} + \beta_3\%\text{Hispanic} + \beta_4\%\text{Asian} + Z + \epsilon$.
  • Race × Income interactions: add terms like $\text{Income} \times \%\text{Black}$ to test whether income effects differ by race/ethnicity.
  • Utility territory fixed effects: include dummies for PG&E, SCE, SDG&E, LADWP (and/or others) to capture policy/infrastructure differences.
  • Infrastructure controls: add renewable capacity per capita and total power-plant capacity per capita (from EIA power plants) to test whether disparities persist even where infrastructure is strong.
  • Clustered / robust SE: cluster standard errors by utility territory to address within-utility correlation.

  • Interactions: [which race×income terms you include; interpret via marginal effects plots]

6 Conclusion

This project provides a data-driven look at DER deployment patterns and the socioeconomic and geographic factors associated with adoption in California.
